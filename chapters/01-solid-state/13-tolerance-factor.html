Goldschmidt tolerance factor

Chapter 1 · Solid state and crystal structures

The Goldschmidt tolerance factor (t) is a dimensionless number used to predict the stability of a crystal and its symmetry in ABO3 perovskites. It relates the ionic radii of the A-site cation (rA), the B-site cation (rB) and the anion (rO).

t = (rA + rO) / [ √2 (rB + rO) ]

When t deviates from the ideal value 1, the crystal lattice experiences a steric mismatch, driving structural distortion to lower-symmetry phases.

tStructureWhyExample
> 1Hexagonal or tetragonalA-site cation too large; A site overbondedBaTiO3 (t ≈ 1.06), BaNiO3
0.9 to 1CubicA, B and O packed ideally; BO6 octahedra untiltedSrTiO3 (t ≈ 1.00), CaTiO3 (t ≈ 0.97, orthorhombic despite t near 1)
0.71 to 0.9Orthorhombic / rhombohedralA-site cation too small; BO6 octahedra tilt and rotateGdFeO3 (t ≈ 0.88)
About 0.8Different structuresPerovskite framework gives way to ilmenite-type structures; the boundary is not sharpMgTiO3 (t = 0.81), FeTiO3 (t ≈ 0.82)

Checked against the standard table. The ranges in the table are a guide, not a sharp boundary. The notes give collapse below t = 0.8 or above t = 1.05, with MgTiO3 (t = 0.81 in the notes) as a non-perovskite example. That limit is arguably closer to where the real ilmenites fall than the 0.71 boundary is: MgTiO3 (t = 0.81) and FeTiO3 (t ≈ 0.82) are both ilmenite, even though they lie inside the nominal perovskite range. CaTiO3 itself is orthorhombic, not cubic, at room temperature, despite t ≈ 0.97.
